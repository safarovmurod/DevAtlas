## Per-day comparison

### Day 24 — Measures and unintended incentives (management-objectives-control)
- S1 "A measure … does not replace the objective" and the plate-count speed-vs-quality example are original teaching framing; nothing in them contradicts the OpenStax page's objective-anchored MBO process (goals → action plan → self-monitoring with feedback).
- S2's arithmetic checks out (18/20 = 90%; undefined 0/0 in the question options matches the source-neutral statement that no distributions means no rate). S3's balancing-constraint and "one short run does not establish sustained improvement" are consistent with — though stricter than — the page's caution that MBO effects vary with commitment and horizon. S4's measurement hygiene (definition, period, source, limits; missing data is not zero) does not contradict the page.
- Coverage note, not a conflict: the fetched page discusses MBO limitations (short-term horizon, commitment, gainsharing) but does not present explicit "unintended incentive / measure gaming" content; the day's theme is an original application over fictional data and does not attribute it to the source. Questions and practice stay inside the fictional record-office case, so no real staff are ranked and no measured gain is claimed.

### Day 25 — Changing plans and priorities (management-project-control)
- S2 (record change request, affected materials, timing effects, decision owner; keep the old plan as previous version) matches GovS 002 §7.7 (record and define change requests, assess impact, version control in §7.9) and §7.8 (status and version per element).
- S3 (owner compares rescheduling / reduced safe scope / agreed help; an authorized person confirms; communicate the active version; label unapproved change as proposed) matches §7.7 authorization and communication requirements and §4.3 delegated decision authority. Q3's "proposed; awaiting the owner's decision" is consistent.
- S4 ("do not buy resources without authority"; record excluded scope rather than hide it) matches §4.3 expenditure controls and §7.7 controlled scope changes. Q1's "all related addresses and agreements" matches §7.7's group control of interrelated deliverables. No conflict.

### Day 26 — Quality and feedback from the team (management-project-control, management-feedback)
- S1 "Quality means fitness for purpose and agreed requirements; checking an output differs from counting hours spent" aligns directly with GovS 002 §8.2 ("outputs are fit for purpose … meet expectations or stated needs") and §8.6 verification vs validation.
- S2's expected/observed/gap record and the "requirement remains unmet" answer (Q1) are consistent with §8.6 and §8.3's requirement that requirements be current, consistent and validated; the recheck of dependent notices (Q2) is consistent with §7.8 two-way traceability.
- S3–S4's feedback handling (ask about the step and version; one opinion does not describe everyone; prepare questions rather than fabricate feedback; do not dismiss inconvenient feedback) is an original application consistent with OpenStax 8.3's accuracy/credibility/fairness evaluation model; the day does not quote the page or reuse its exhibit. No conflict.

### Day 27 — A retrospective of the work process (management-retrospective, management-project-control)
- S1 (review how work happened to improve the next cycle; distinct from output acceptance) matches the Atlassian play's improvement-not-blame purpose and GovS §8.10 learning from experience / §6.4.8 closure review; the two sources are complementary and the day does not conflate them.
- S2 (observation vs possible explanation; check timestamps; preserve helpful actions; review successes and difficulties) matches the play's gather-then-find-patterns steps. S3 (one improvement with owner and next check; "one feasible action is better than ten promises without owners") matches the play's action items with owners and deadlines; Q2's conversion of "communicate better" into action/owner/check matches.
- S4 and Q3 keep the solo-author boundary (scenario analysis, not a team meeting; no invented voices) — consistent with the play being a team exercise and with the course's honesty limits. No conflict.

### Day 28 — Assembling a fictional team project (management-project-control, management-team-roles)
- S1–S2 (goals link to tasks, tasks to owners, checks to outcomes; package of brief, dependency plan, handover, update, risk log, decision; mark current versions; venue confirmation precedes an addressed poster) match GovS §7.2.4 (dependencies defined, single-point accountability), §7.8 (status and version per element), §7.9 and the §3.3 work-package definition. Q1's "consistency check of active versions" matches §8.3's "mutually consistent" requirements plus §7.8/§7.9.
- S3–S4 (disruption test; authorized decision route; rubric self-review citing passages) are consistent with §7.7/§4.3 and §8.6 verification/validation methods (simulation, test). The Atlassian roles play supports the tasks-to-owners and missing-owner defects framing; its seven-step table method is not reproduced or claimed. No conflict.

### Day 29 — Checking risks and communication (management-project-control, management-accessible-events)
- S1's "paper rehearsal" (test a plan against a stated disruption without a real incident) is course terminology consistent with GovS §8.6 verification/validation methods (simulation, demonstration, test, pilots); the day does not claim GovS prescribes rehearsals.
- S2–S3 (signal → coordinator → authorized decision; backup route rather than invented consent; pause dependent work without a decision; check that people without the main channel still receive critical changes) match §4.1.3/§4.3/§6.4.4 authority limits and escalation, and §7.13 communications that are "acceptable and accessible to the recipients" with feedback mechanisms. Q2's answer follows.
- S4 and Q1 (a sent message does not prove it was read and understood; do not call channels accessible without checking) are consistent with §7.13 effectiveness measures and the WAI page's requirement to verify platform accessibility and offer multiple connection options; day 29's offline-route criterion makes no accessibility certification claim. No conflict.

### Day 30 — Defending the project and planning next steps (management-project-control, management-feedback, management-accessible-events, management-retrospective)
- S1–S2 (defend through work, resources, outcomes and constraints rather than page count; answer why this option, what if it fails, what remains unproven; tie answers to criteria and package extracts) match GovS §4.3 (options assessed against defined criteria; business case justification) and §7.6's viability questions; Q1 and Q3's "objective and an honest revision" align.
- S3 (one day-27 improvement with action, owner and observation time; planned mentor read ≠ independent assessment received) matches the Atlassian follow-up principle and OpenStax 8.3's source-credibility emphasis; Q2's "planned; no independent assessment yet" keeps the boundary.

- RU/TG bodies and option strings were checked only structurally (RU/EN length ratios 0.96–1.11 across all 28 sections, three options per question, valid `correctIndex` per question); a sentence-level translation-quality review is separate work and was not performed here.
- GovS 002 clauses not used by days 24–30 (procurement, finance, health and safety, environment, portfolio practices, etc.) were read but not compared in depth, since the days make no claims from them.
- The review checked factual/conceptual agreement between day text and sources; pedagogy quality, language polish and cross-day coherence were out of scope. Per the task boundary, nothing was edited in the course JSON: all observations below are recorded for the coordinator, not fixed.
- As with the rest of the course, nothing in these days proves real team outcomes, real event operations or independently assessed mastery; the days' own texts already state these limits.
